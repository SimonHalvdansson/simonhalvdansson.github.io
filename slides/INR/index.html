1Background

Neural networks can approximate any function

Universal approximation theorem
For every continuous \( f : [0,1]^n \to \R \) and every \( \varepsilon > 0 \) there is a network \[ \mathcal{N}(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{N} a_j\, \sigma(w_j \cdot x + b_j) \] such that \( |f(x) - \mathcal{N}(x)| < \varepsilon \) for all \( x \).
target \( f \) ReLU network \( \mathcal{N} \)
In reality, \( N \) needs to be very large.
  • ~1.5 min. Most of you know this theorem: one hidden layer can approximate any continuous function.
  • Step 1: with ReLU every neuron is a hinge; three hinges add up to a piecewise linear fit.
  • Step 2: more neurons, better fit (N = 3 → 48, max error 0.54 → 0.01).
  • Step 3: the catch — the theorem says nothing about how many neurons.

1Background

Implicit neural representation

Explicit: a grid of numbers
128 × 128 × 3 = 49,152 numbers
Implicit: a function \( f_\theta \)
Fit with gradient descent: \( \displaystyle \min_\theta \sum_{i} \big\| f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i) - \mathbf{y}_i \big\|^2 \) (pixel positions \( \mathbf{x}_i \), colors \( \mathbf{y}_i \))
The weights \( \theta \) are the image: query it anywhere, at any resolution.
  • ~1.5 min. Explicit: store every pixel (49k numbers here). Implicit: store a function you can ask "what colour at (x, y)?".
  • "Implicit" because the object is defined through a function rather than listed.
  • Training is plain least squares over the pixels — like any regression.

3Fourier features

Recall: Fourier series

\( \displaystyle f(x) \approx a_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \big( a_k \cos(2\pi k x) + b_k \sin(2\pi k x) \big) \)
\( \displaystyle \mathbf{z}(t) = \sum_{k} r_k \begin{pmatrix} \cos(kt + \varphi_k) \\ \sin(kt + \varphi_k) \end{pmatrix} \in \R^2 \)
Sinusoids are a very efficient vocabulary. Idea: let the MLP build its function out of sinusoids.
  • ~1 min. Reminder: a periodic signal is a sum of sines and cosines; coefficients decay fast for smooth signals.
  • Click: same idea in 2D — a closed curve drawn by 40 rotating circles (80 numbers).
  • Message: sinusoids are a compact vocabulary for signals.

4Applications

Why are INRs cool?

Continuous

Evaluate anywhere, good interpolation.

Differentiable

Exact derivatives \( \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f_\theta \) by autodiff: normals, PDE residuals (PINNs).

Plugs into physics

Fit through a forward model: \( \min_\theta \| A(f_\theta) - y \|^2 \).

Compact

Store \( \theta \), not samples. Compress images, textures, simulation and weather data.

  • ~1 min. Continuous: no resolution — ask for any point.
  • Differentiable: derivatives with respect to the input for free (normals, PDE residuals → PINNs).
  • Plugs into physics: we can fit it to indirect measurements → CT next.
  • Compact: with the right size, the network is smaller than the data (e.g. weather/climate data) → textures at the end.

4Applications

Solving the inverse problem

Inverse problem: we measure \( y = A(\mu) \) for a known forward model \( A \) and want \( \mu \) back.
Fitting an image\( \displaystyle \min_\theta \; \big\| {\color{#2a78d6} f_\theta} - y \big\|^2 \)
Inverse problem\( \displaystyle \min_\theta \; \big\| {\color{#b07800} A}\big({\color{#2a78d6} f_\theta}\big) - y \big\|^2 \)
\( f_\theta \) is our current best guess of the object.
We have a differentiable model for \( A \).
Gradients flow through \( A \) back to \( \theta \).
  • ~1.5 min. Before: compare the network directly to the data. Click: now the data are projections, so project the guess the same way and compare.
  • Click: because f_θ is continuous, "integrate along a ray" = evaluate the network at points on the ray and sum.
  • The gradient goes back through A to the weights. No ground-truth image needed anywhere.

4Applications

Neural texture compression

A material on a 3D model is many textures stacked: 9+ channels at 4096 × 4096.

  • Albedo (3) base colour
  • Normal (3) surface direction: fake bumps
  • Roughness (1) matte vs. shiny
  • Ambient occlusion (1) shadow in crevices
  • Height (1) displacement
\( f_\theta(u, v, \text{lod}) \)

One small network per material (latent grid + tiny MLP) decodes all channels at once, per pixel, inside the shader.

  • ~1.5 min. A 3D model's material stores albedo, normals, roughness, AO, height … (9+ channels at 4K).
  • Click: together they make the material (relit live here).
  • Click: NVIDIA (SIGGRAPH 2023): one tiny network per material, decoded per pixel in the shader, exploiting correlations across channels and mip levels.
  • Click: same quality as block compression at ~1/3.5 of the bits (≈ 1.1 vs 3.9 bits per value), or 16× more texels for the same memory.
5Summary

Takeaways

  1. 1
    Images, sound, video, shapes and physical fields are all functions \( \R^n \to \R^m \).
  2. 2
    An INR stores such a function as a small neural network \( f_\theta \).
  3. 3
    Plain MLPs are biased towards low frequencies. Fourier features fix this in three lines of code.
  4. 4
    INRs are compact, continuous, differentiable — and can be fitted through physics: \( \min_\theta \|A(f_\theta) - y\|^2 \).
  • ~1 min. Recap the four points; then the ideas closer to home — invite discussion.
